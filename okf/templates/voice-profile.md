# Brand voice profile

This file describes how one organization sounds. Loaded alongside the Dignified Language Standard, it tells a model what to prefer, not what it is allowed to do. The standard sets the floor; this profile sets the personality.

How to use it: copy this file, keep the frontmatter, and replace every `>` instruction line with your answer. Delete the instruction lines when you are done, or leave them; models ignore them. Fields marked **required** must be filled for the profile to work. Everything else improves the result but can be skipped; write `not specified` and the model will use sensible defaults.

If you would rather be asked than fill in a form, paste `voice-interview.md` into Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini and it will produce this file for you. GoldenDoodle AI can also build it from your website at goldendoodleai.com.

## 1. Identity (required)

**Organization name:**
> The official name, as it appears on your website.

**Voice name:**
> A label for this profile. Most organizations start with one, such as "Main voice." Create a second profile later if you need a distinct donor voice or internal voice.

**Website:**
> Your primary public site. A model reading this profile may use it to check facts.

**Organization type:**
> Choose one: nonprofit or community-based, association or membership organization, healthcare, government, education, environment, business, other. If "other," say what.

**Service area:**
> How you describe the region or community you serve, in your own words. Example: "Sacramento County and the surrounding foothills" or "clinicians practicing in California."

## 2. Mission, vision, values

**Mission statement:**
> Paste it verbatim from your site. Do not paraphrase; the model will quote it.

**Vision statement:**
> Verbatim if you have one. Leave `not specified` if you do not.

**Core values:**
> Three to six, as you name them. Example: "Dignity, Accountability, Belonging." If you have a sentence for each, include it.

**About us, in one paragraph:**
> The paragraph you would put at the bottom of a press release. Verbatim if it exists.

## 3. Audience

**Primary audience:**
> Describe one person, not a segment. Who most often reads what you write? Example: "A program director at a mid-sized human services nonprofit, reading on her phone between meetings."

**What they are dealing with:**
> The pressures or problems they bring to you. Example: "Too little staff time, funders asking for outcomes data, and a board that wants everything to sound upbeat."

**The relationship you want with them:**
> Choose the closest, or write your own: trusted neighbor, expert guide, peer, advocate on their side, steady institution.

## 4. Personality

**Primary archetype:**
> Choose one. Each is a short description of how the voice carries itself.
> - Caregiver: warm, protective, service-first. "We're here for you."
> - Sage: knowledgeable, measured, evidence-led. "Here's what we know."
> - Advocate: principled, determined, speaks for others. "This has to change."
> - Everyperson: plainspoken, relatable, no pretense. "We're in this together."
> - Innocent: hopeful, optimistic, simple. "Good things are possible."
> - Ruler: authoritative, orderly, sets the standard. "This is how it's done."
> - Creator: imaginative, builds new things. "Let's make something."
> - Explorer: curious, independent, pushes outward. "There's more to find."
> - Magician: visionary, makes the unlikely happen. "Watch what's possible."
> - Outlaw: challenges the status quo. "The old way isn't working."
> - Jester: playful, light, disarming. "Let's not take ourselves too seriously."
> - Lover: intimate, passionate, relationship-first. "You matter to us."
> Most mission-driven organizations land on Caregiver, Sage, Advocate, or Everyperson.

**Secondary archetype:**
> Optional. A second archetype that shows up in some contexts. Example: "Sage primary, Everyperson secondary." Write `none` if you do not want one.

**Tone scales:**
> Give a number from 1 to 5 on each scale. 1 is the left word, 5 is the right word, 3 is the middle.
> - Casual (1) to Formal (5): 1 reads like a text to a friend; 5 reads like a letter from counsel. Most nonprofits sit at 2 or 3.
> - Playful (1) to Serious (5): 1 uses humor freely; 5 never does. Organizations in high-stakes work usually sit at 4.
> - Modern (1) to Traditional (5): 1 uses current language and short forms; 5 uses established, institutional phrasing.
> - Collaborative (1) to Authoritative (5): 1 says "we" and asks; 5 says "you should" and instructs.

## 5. Vocabulary

**Words and phrases we use:**
> Terms that are distinctly yours, with a note on when. Example: "'neighbors' for the people we serve; 'the campus' for our main site; 'partners' rather than 'donors' in public copy."

**Words and phrases we avoid:**
> Terms you never want in your copy. This list adds to the standard's vocabulary files; it cannot remove anything from them. Example: "'clients' (we say 'participants'), 'beneficiaries', 'give back'."

**Glossary:**
> Terms with a meaning specific to your organization, and what they mean. Example: "'Navigator': a staff member assigned to walk with a participant through intake."

## 6. Style

**Reading level:**
> Choose one.
> - Simple: grades 6 to 8. Short sentences, everyday words. The recommended default for anything read by the public, and the SAMHSA-aligned choice for trauma-informed work.
> - Standard: grades 9 to 12. Fine for member newsletters and general professional audiences.
> - Technical: college and above. Only for content written for specialists, such as clinical or policy audiences.

**Verbosity:**
> Choose one.
> - Concise: say it and stop. Best for social, email, and alerts.
> - Balanced: enough context to be clear, no more. The usual default.
> - Expositional: full explanation with background. For reports, articles, and testimony.

**Contractions:**
> yes or no. "Yes" reads as human; "no" reads as formal. Most organizations should say yes.

**Press release boilerplate:**
> The "About [Organization]" paragraph, verbatim, if you issue press releases. Otherwise `not specified`.

**Compliance disclaimers:**
> Any language you are required to include, verbatim. Example: "This material is for informational purposes only and is not medical advice." Otherwise `not specified`.

## 7. Scenarios

**When we share a win, we frame it as:**
> Choose one. This sets the shape of every success announcement.
> - We achieved this together: collective credit, community first. "Together, we..."
> - Here are the outcomes: lead with numbers and evidence. "In 2026, 214 families..."
> - We are thrilled to announce: warm, celebratory, high energy.
> - This proves change is possible: the win as evidence for the mission. "This shows what happens when..."

**When we address a problem or crisis, we lead with:**
> Choose one. This sets the opening move when something has gone wrong.
> - Radical transparency: own it immediately, focus on accountability.
> - Reassuring and steady: calm, clear instructions, focus on safety. The recommended default for trauma-informed organizations.
> - Formal protocol: "We are aware of the situation and are investigating."
> - Rallying cry: the setback as a reason to push harder.
> - Deep empathy: grief and presence first, information second.

## 8. Signature

**When we sound like ourselves, we sound like:**
> One sentence. Example: "A neighbor who happens to know how the system works, explaining it over coffee."

**Anything that overrides all of the above:**
> A rule that wins every time. Example: "Always 'survivor,' never 'victim'" or "Never use exclamation points." Write `none` if you have nothing.

## 9. Layers

**Standard:** Dignified Language (required; always on)

**Trauma-Informed layer:**
> yes or no. Say yes if the people you serve have been affected by trauma, which includes most human services, health, housing, and family-serving organizations. If yes, this layer sits above everything in this profile and cannot be relaxed by it.

**Sector vocabulary in use:**
> List the files from `okf/standard/vocabulary/` that apply: housing, substance-use, mental-health, disability, non-pathologizing, reporting-standards. Or `none`.

**Human voice module:**
> yes or no. Say yes to suppress common AI writing patterns (banned words, em dashes, formulaic structure). Recommended for any organization that drafts with AI.
